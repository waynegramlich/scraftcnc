
Cutting speed depends heavily on the combination of the drill material
(High-Speed Steel [HSS] vs. Carbide) and the workpiece material:
• Aluminum / Brass / Acetal (Plastics): ~250 SFM (HSS) up to higher speeds for carbide
• Mild Steel / Soft Cast Iron: ~100 SFM (HSS)
• Medium/Hard Cast Iron: ~80 SFM (HSS)
• Tool Steel / Alloy Steel: ~40 SFM (HSS)
• Stainless Steel / Titanium / Hardened Steel: ~20 SFM (HSS) or conservative lower ranges
• Tip: Carbide tooling generally allows you to multiply HSS surface speeds by roughly 2.5x, while cobalt or high-moly tool steels handle tough work-hardening materials better than standard HSS.

SFM: Surface Feet per Minute (specified by manufacturer)
RPM: = Rotations Per Minute (1/min.)
D: Drill Diameter (inches)


How IPR is Determined in Practice

1. Rule of Thumb (Ferrous / Standard Drills):
   * A common shop guideline sets feed rate as roughly 0.001in per revolution
     for every 1/16 inch of drill diameter (adjusted up or down based on
     hardness and depth):
     * IPR ~= Drill Diameter x Material Factor
2. From various manufacture catalogs:
   * Under 1/8" diameter: 0.001in - 0.003in per rev.
   * 1/8" to 1/4" diameter: 0.002in - 0.006" per rev.
   * 1/4" to 1/2" diameter: 0.004in - 0.010in per rev
   * 1/2" to 1" diameter: 0.007in - 0.015in per rev.
3. Connecting to IPM (Inches Per Minute):
   * Once you choose an IPR value from a catalog or rule of thumb, you multiply
     it by the calculated RPM to find your linear feed rate in inches per
     minute:
     *  IPM = IPR * RPM

RPM = (SFM * 12) / (pi * D)
IPM = RPM * IPR
IPS = IPM / 60
